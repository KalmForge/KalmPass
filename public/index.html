Zero-knowledge by construction

A password manager that cannot read your passwords.

Your vault is encrypted in your browser, with a key made from a master password we never receive. What reaches our servers is ciphertext. There is no copy of your key in our database, no override, and nothing useful to steal.

Two keys open your vault. We hold neither.

Everything in KalmPass is encrypted under one random vault key. That key is wrapped twice — once by your master password, once by your Recovery Key — and only the wrapped copies are stored. Either secret opens your vault. Nothing on our side does.

The awkward questions.

What happens if I forget my master password?

You use your Recovery Key — a 125-bit key issued once when you sign up, which you save offline. It opens your vault and lets you set a new password, with nothing lost. If you lose both your password and that key, your data is genuinely gone: there is no key in existence that can decrypt it. That is the direct cost of us not holding one, and we would rather be honest about it than pretend otherwise.

Could you be forced to hand over my passwords?

We can be compelled to hand over what we hold, which is encrypted blobs and the account email. We cannot be compelled to produce a key we have never had. Your master password is not transmitted, so it is not ours to give up.

What if KalmPass is breached?

An attacker with our entire database gets ciphertext. Vault contents are encrypted in your browser under your key, and then encrypted again by our servers under a key stored outside the database. Even email addresses are stored as keyed hashes rather than in the clear.

What if KalmPass disappears?

Export an encrypted backup whenever you like — it is a single JSON file protected by a passphrase you choose, and it restores without us. We would rather you had one than depended on us being here forever.

Is there a browser extension?

Not yet. The web vault works on every device, installs as an app from your browser, and copies to the clipboard with an auto-clear timer. Autofill is the next thing we want to build.
